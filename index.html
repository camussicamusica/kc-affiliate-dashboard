Choose products she can demonstrate well, then measure real commission per hour. Product price, bonus percentage, sales volume, and filming effort all matter.

Read the full research

What the research found

Amazon’s ordinary onsite rates currently differ by category: Home/Furniture 3%, Kitchen 1.25%, and Toys/Baby 0.5%. Ordinary offsite rates include Kitchen 4.5% and Home/Toys/Baby 3%. Amazon determines the category and qualifying sale; onsite commission is tied to the same product variation. Creator Ads and campaign bonuses are separate. [A1] [A2]

ViralVue’s calculator uses assumptions, including an editable default of one attributed sale per 100 product sales. Delivery time and remaining campaign days change its result. A large forecast is a scenario to examine, not KC’s expected payout. [V1]

What creators reported

In the Platinum thread, one creator said delegating editing, uploads, and completion notices gave her evenings back. A separate turnaround discussion included negotiating two weeks when a brand requested seven days. Those are workable ideas to test; neither thread measures how much extra profit delegation caused. [C1] [C3]

What we recommend for KC

  1. Apply the existing product rules first: useful products, up to the current $500 research ceiling, an appropriate campaign, and real product access. Self-funded purchases remain paused.
  2. Rank the remaining options by expected testing/filming effort, likely customer demand, category rate, and usable campaign days after delivery and production.
  3. Agree the turnaround before requesting a sample. Limit incoming products to what KC can actually finish.
  4. Batch similar demonstrations. Delegate preparation or editing only where it saves more than it costs; KC retains the real testing and final accuracy check.
  5. At 30, 60, and 90 days, compare commission after returns and direct costs with KC’s hours. Record Sofia’s hours separately so the total workload remains visible.

Example, not an earnings prediction: a review producing $30 after direct costs in one hour is a better use of KC’s time than one producing $45 in three hours. We need actual product reports and production times to rank KC’s opportunities.

02STRATEGY + ACCOUNT DATA

What strategies could help KC reach Platinum?

Build on the products already working, improve product selection, and track the weekly points gap. Platinum needs 1,000 quarterly points; the missing piece is KC’s current pace and balance.

Read the full research

Start with the real goal

In the October 7 call, KC described getting close to Platinum in earlier quarters. The task is to identify a manageable improvement in sales and production. It is not enough to tell her the threshold, and there is no supported “upload this many videos” shortcut. [K1]

Amazon awards one point per $100 of eligible shipped revenue and one per 1,000 clicks, with a maximum of 49 click points each quarter. Platinum starts at 1,000. Points expire quarterly; a tier earned is retained through the following quarter. Without click points the threshold corresponds to $100,000 of eligible shipped revenue; with the full click allowance it corresponds to $95,100. These are customer sales, not KC’s income or purchase budget. [A3]

Five strategies to use or test

  1. Audit the products already earning. Start with the ten strongest products in a recent Amazon report. Check whether the exact item is still available, the tagged product is accurate, and the video answers a real buying question. Fix a genuine mismatch; do not attach an old demo to an untested replacement. A practitioner’s catalog-audit advice supports this starting point, while Amazon’s tagging rules limit what can be changed. [P1] [A8]
  2. Choose products where a demonstration helps a purchase decision. Test suitable home/family products that raise practical questions about size, setup, cleaning, noise, storage, or use. Compare likely shipped sales with production time. A more expensive item can generate more sales revenue per order but may sell less often or take longer to review. Public creators recommend useful, concise demonstrations and manageable competition; this remains experience-based advice. [C6]
  3. Give content enough time to work. Build a steady pipeline early in the quarter. Account for sample delivery, real testing, editing, approval, and campaign expiry. Late uploads cannot guarantee a last-minute tier increase. The exact time to placement and sales varies; we are not adopting a fixed waiting period. [P1] [V1]
  4. Protect production capacity and brand relationships. Agree a realistic turnaround, decline unsuitable samples, and finish the work already promised. Test delegating editing before raising sample volume. Creators who reached Platinum describe these habits, but their posts do not prove which habit caused the result. [C1] [C3] [C4]
  5. Learn from the winners each week. Compare shipped revenue, commission after returns, and hours by product. Make the next batch more like KC’s successful use cases. A new video should answer a different useful question; repeated uploads alone are not a validated growth strategy.

Turn the goal into smaller milestones

Record the current points, revenue points, click points, quarter end date, and points gained over the last two weeks. Then calculate remaining points ÷ weeks left. Compare that weekly requirement with KC’s recent pace. Use the Stars dashboard as the score of record; do not assume every number in a separate earnings report counts identically.

09UPDATED AI GUIDANCE

What are the rules for images, AI, links, and disclosures?

Use KC’s real experience and assets she has rights to use. Disclose affiliate/sample ties and AI-generated content as required. Amazon videos and individual campaigns have additional conditions.

Read the full research

Disclosures and honest experience

Amazon requires the identification “As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.” FTC guidance also requires a clear disclosure with the relevant endorsement, including free-product relationships; a profile-only note is insufficient. Put video disclosures in the video where viewers can notice them. Claims must reflect actual experience. [A5] [F1]

A customer star review is different from disclosed Influencer content. Do not promise a compensated Amazon customer review for a sample. [A10]

Images and product information

Use original material or assets with the necessary permission. Amazon’s Program Content license limits alterations and use; price/availability display has specific conditions. Its policies also restrict artificial traffic, incentives, and self-purchases. A seller’s listing image is not automatically permission to reuse or transform it. [A6]

AI: a correction to the earlier answer

Amazon’s current Creator University FAQ permits some AI assistance, such as post headers or text overlays, and says AI-created content must clearly disclose both sponsorship and AI generation. It also says Amazon data may not be run through AI/automation services, and lists restrictions involving PAAPI and AI training. The precise application to a particular tool needs checking; the earlier broad suggestion to use AI did not cover this. [A9]

For KC, start with her own notes and original assets, verify every factual statement, and apply the required disclosures. Do not invent a demonstration or imply she tested an item she has not. Keep a proposed “find” distinct from an actual review. This is our practical application of the guidance and KC’s preference for real reviews. [K1]

For uploaded Amazon shoppable videos

  • Tag only clearly featured products, with the correct item/variation.
  • Disclose a gifted or paid relationship in the video.
  • Leave out prices, promotions, and time-sensitive claims.
  • A shipping/packaging-only video is not allowed.
  • Do not promote external destinations that take viewers away from Amazon.

The livestream rules are separate, including their treatment of prices. Do not copy a social sale caption into an uploaded Amazon video without adapting it. [A8]

Creator Connections: what the full guide established

The 24-page guide explicitly describes onsite and offsite bonus reporting. Onsite bonuses therefore exist in general. It also requires the selling merchant to match the campaign initiator, warns that budget figures can lag, and distinguishes Affiliate+ from Sponsored Products for Creators CPC. Those findings do not verify a particular future campaign. [A12]

The guide says content-link submission is optional in several places, while one live campaign inspected on October 14 required it. Follow the actual request; seek clarification if relying on a conflicting exception. Current terms require truthful content, rights, disclosure, and compliance with the accepted request. CPC-specific conditions should not be generalized to every affiliate workflow. [A11] [A12] [L1]

A prior tracking warning remains a historical unresolved observation. Sofia’s latest instruction is to continue routine product research and defer troubleshooting unless she asks. This website does not turn that warning into a new research blocker or promise any campaign payout. [K1]

Build a usable inventory

Group existing tested products and footage into home reset/cleaning, family routines, children’s play, and seasonal gifting. Seasonality and placement are explanations to investigate, not conclusions from a graph alone.
