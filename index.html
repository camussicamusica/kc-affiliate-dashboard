How can KC earn more per review without taking on too much?

Choose products she can genuinely demonstrate, then track real commission per hour after product costs and production time. More videos alone do not prove better returns.

Read the full answer

Why this answer: Amazon pays different rates for ordinary onsite video and offsite affiliate traffic. The category, exact ASIN variation, campaign terms, and actual shipped purchases determine earnings. A tool’s forecast is a scenario, not KC’s expected payout.

What to do: Keep KC’s product-fit and campaign gates, then rank eligible items by how long real testing, filming, editing, and uploading will take—and by how many campaign days remain after delivery. Batch similar demos when that saves KC time. Measure realized commission at 30, 60, and 90 days, subtract direct costs, and divide by KC’s hours. Track Sofia’s time separately.

The practical question is: which product delivers a worthwhile return for the work KC actually has time to do?

02NEEDS ACCOUNT DATA

What would move KC from Gold to Platinum?

Platinum requires 1,000 quarterly points from eligible shipped revenue and clicks. Upload count is not the target. KC’s current point balance is still unknown.

Read the full answer

How points work: Amazon awards one point per $100 in eligible shipped revenue and one per 1,000 clicks, with click points capped at 49 each quarter. Without click points, 1,000 points corresponds to $100,000 of eligible revenue; with the full 49, the revenue portion is 951 points, or $95,100. Those are sales revenue figures, not KC’s commission or a spending target.

What Platinum changes: Published benefits include a storefront badge and earlier access to some event information. The benefits reviewed do not promise a higher standard commission rate. Points reset by quarter, while a tier earned can continue into the next quarter.

To calculate KC’s real gap, we need her current-quarter point total and the revenue/click breakdown from her Creator Stars view.

Should KC message brands at a certain hour?

We found no proven best hour. Send qualified first messages during normal working hours. Follow up only when the campaign and tool allow it; compare response rates before changing her schedule.

Read the full answer

What we found: KC asked this question in the creator group. Replies suggested different hours, including late evening and early morning, but they were personal experiences without a controlled comparison. What the tool requires: ViralVue’s documented follow-up requires an unanswered first message sent through its tool, at least 24 hours elapsed, no earlier follow-up, and an active campaign with remaining budget. Its follow-up allowance is separate; KC’s exact account allowance has not been verified.

Send good initial messages when KC is working. If timing still matters, compare similar products and templates across two sending windows, then look at replies and sample agreements after seven days.

Can Friday research filter for US sellers?

We found no seller-country field in ViralVue’s current Product Findr or Campaign Findr web filters. Check each seller’s business location manually; US marketplace availability is not proof.

Read the full answer

What was checked: The live Product Findr and Campaign Findr web filters and column lists did not expose a seller-country or business-location field. ViralVue’s US marketplace support means it researches Amazon.com; it does not establish where a seller’s business is based.

Practical method: Open the seller information, look for a verifiable business address or brand company details, and record that source beside the candidate. Keep business location separate from “ships from Amazon,” a US delivery address, or a US manufacturing claim. Sellers may also be resellers, so confirm the seller associated with the campaign.

Mark uncertain cases “location unverified” rather than counting them in a verified US-seller Friday list. We did not establish whether every ViralVue extension feature has the same limits.

09USE NOW

What are the rules for images, AI, links, and disclosures?

Use KC’s real experience and assets she can use, disclose affiliate or sample ties clearly, and avoid invented product claims. Amazon videos have extra rules: tag only featured items and omit prices or promotions.

Read the full answer

Affiliate and disclosure basics: Amazon’s agreement requires the Associate identification on the relevant presence. The FTC treats a free sample as a financial connection too; disclose it clearly with the endorsement, including in video where viewers can notice it. A profile-only note is not enough. Do not offer a compensated Amazon customer star review in exchange for a product.

Content rules: KC can use AI to organize notes or draft captions for her correction, but it cannot invent a demonstration or imply she tested something she has not. Use her own or properly licensed assets. For Amazon shoppable videos, tag only clearly featured products, disclose a gifted/paid connection in the video, and leave out prices, promotions, external traffic prompts, and packaging-only footage.

Creator Connections has its own campaign brief and link rules. Onsite bonuses are possible generally, but the live campaign’s content-link instruction and tracking warning still need account-specific clearance.
